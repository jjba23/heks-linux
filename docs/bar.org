* The bar

The bar is a menu bar, not a floating capsule: flush with the top edge, the full
width of the screen, and one row of text over a material the wallpaper shows
through. The proportions were measured from the desktop this one was drawn from
rather than guessed, and then adjusted in the one place the measurement did not
apply - this row carries a mark and icons taller than its text, so
~whelk-bar-height~ is 32 pixels where the reference's 13 pixel text filled 59%
of 22.

It is one surface of the shell, rendered by ~whelk.scm~ into
~/.config/whelk/settings.scm~ and installed by ~maak install-whelk~. Everything
below is a name in that module, which is the reason the bar can be argued with
rather than only liked or replaced.

** The mark, and the machine's own menu

At the far left, where the reference keeps its mark, this bar wears the
machine's own: an empty star drawn as an outline, quiet at 16 pixels and
readable in one ink. It is the ~start-module~, and it opens one menu of what
this machine is, how to make it yours, and every way out of the session, with
About This System on its first row.

** File, Edit and View, and the status items

Beside the mark is the focused application's own File, Edit and View
(~menu-module~, with the application's name and the shortcuts shown). Everything
after that is a status item on the right, in the order the bar this replaces
kept: what is playing and what is copied, the things this desktop opens, the
hardware, the network, the things that arrive, and then the magnifier and the
clock at the far right where a menu bar puts them.

Every row carries two clicks - a left click that opens the thing and a right
click that does the faster thing to it - so no reading on this bar is one you
can only look at. The rows take no palette of their own: the bar carries one ink
and one accent, and a row that picked its own colour would be a row that stopped
following the theme.

** The shelf behind the chevron

Readings looked at once in a while are not on the row: the load, the ways into
the desktop's own views (the keyboard reference, the wallpapers, the palettes,
the characters), the keyboard layout and the brightness are behind the chevron
at the end, in ~whelk-overflow-rows~. They are built with the bar rather than on
demand, so a reading keeps updating and its clicks keep working while the shelf
is shut.

Folding them away starts closed (~overflow-expanded?~ is ~#f~), and that is what
keeps the right half the width a menu bar has. A tile for every program is a bar
where the clock - the one reading that has to be found without looking - ends up
somewhere in the middle.

** The material

Under it all is what the reference bar was made of: the wallpaper blurred and
saturated by the compositor, a thin veil tinted halfway between the palette and
a neutral grey, a lit top edge, a hairline at its foot, and text shadowed so it
stays readable over any wallpaper. The veil is three numbers in ~whelk.scm~, not
a named theme:

| *Name*                  | *What it is*                                                  |
|-------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| ~whelk-bar-tint~         | How far the veil leans to the palette rather than the neutral. |
| ~whelk-bar-neutral-light~ | The neutral a light material is mixed toward.                |
| ~whelk-bar-neutral-dark~  | The dark one.                                                |
| ~whelk-bar-base~         | The colour underneath, before the tint.                      |
| ~whelk-bar-ink~          | The one ink every row is drawn in.                           |
| ~whelk-bar-radius~       | The corner radius of the material.                           |
| ~whelk-extra-css~        | A string of CSS of your own, applied after the theme - ~""~ leaves the theme in charge. |

A machine can soften the shadow or drop the shine without choosing another theme
for the whole bar, because those three strengths are written out in the module
rather than hidden behind a name.

** Why there is no shadow, and why the hairline is the edge

The shadow under the bar is off. A menu bar is the top of the screen, not a
panel floating over it, and on a light palette a shadow darkened the first
fourteen pixels of every window by up to 23% - a rendering artefact, not a
design. The hairline is the whole edge instead, and the windows the bar opens
hang off its bottom edge rather than floating below it.

** Text, rendered the same way everywhere

Text is drawn the way the reference draws it: grayscale antialiasing, light
hinting, no LCD filter. That is set once in ~fonts.conf~ by ~fontconfig.scm~, so
the bar, the launcher, the notifications, the terminal and the editor smooth
their glyphs the same way - instead of only the surfaces GTK happens to own.
[[file:configuration.org][Configuration]] argues that choice through.

** About This Heks

The About dialog is laid out like the reference's: the machine's name, the
system it runs and who is logged in at the top, then Processor, Memory, Startup
Disk, Kernel, Compositor, Palette and Uptime, one line each, with the label in
bold. It is a card the shell draws, not a program of its own, which is why it
knows the palette and the uptime without being told.

** The bar a machine without whelk draws with

whelk is a package this repository builds, so a machine that never built it - or
one that is not Void - has no shell of this kind. ~waybar.scm~ renders the
fallback: a JSONC configuration and a stylesheet from the same palette, a
status bar rather than a menu bar, with the same readings on it. It is not the
same bar and does not pretend to be; it is the bar that means a session still
opens its windows. [[file:desktop.org][The desktop, piece by piece]] has the
module map, and [[file:palettes.org][Palettes]] has the colours both of them
draw with.
