* Music and media

Free music, as in freedom 🎵, with the batteries in the box: PipeWire carries the
sound, MPD owns the library and the playback, RMPC is the client on the desktop,
and Cava is the visualiser inside it. All four are packages, none of them is a
store, and the whole stack is configured by ~rmpc.scm~ and installed by
~maak install-rmpc~.

** MPD owns the sound, the window only asks

The server plays; the client talks to it. That is what makes the music survive a
closed window, a crashed session or a laptop that suspends - the sound belongs to
the daemon, not to the window that started it. RMPC points at an MPD by URL,
~rmpc-mpd-url~, so the same client plays a library on this machine or one on the
network, and ~rmpc-password~ is a parameter like the rest: a machine's own
credential lives in its override file, outside Git.

** RMPC, driven with Emacs' keys

The client is configured with the keys an Emacs user already has in their hands:
~C-n~ and ~C-p~ move, ~C-v~ and ~M-v~ page, ~C-s~ searches, ~C-g~ quits a menu,
~C-x C-c~ leaves, ~C-x C-s~ saves a playlist, ~C-SPC~ sets the mark, ~C-k~ kills
the current row, and ~C-h~ is the help prefix. Keys RMPC binds by default and
Emacs gives no meaning to - ~p~ to toggle pause, ~>~ for the next track - stay
where they were, so nothing is remapped for the sake of remapping.

~rmpc-keybinds~ is the table. What RMPC reads is the rendered ~config.ron~, not a
second copy of the table in prose, which is why [[file:keybindings.org][the keybinding
page]] does not repeat it.

** The layout: tabs, search and the library

Seven tabs, one key each: queue, directories, artists, album artists, albums,
playlists and search. The search and artist views are two forms of their own -
~rmpc-search-and-artists~ - so the library is reachable by typing rather than by
walking a tree, and the queue keeps its place while you do it.

** Cava, inside the player

Cava is the visualiser, and it is configured by RMPC rather than beside it:
~rmpc-cava~ renders the ~cava:~ block into the same file, drawing at 60 frames a
second from a PipeWire capture, and ~rmpc-cava-theme~ gives it the palette's
colours. A machine with no Cava installed has a player without a visualiser,
which is the rule the rest of this desktop follows too.

** Two hooks that make it part of the desktop

RMPC runs a shell script on every song change, and this tree renders two of them:

- ~rmpc-notify~ posts "Now Playing" as a desktop notification with the album art
  RMPC reports, falling back to a bundled picture when a track has none;
- ~rmpc-on-song-change~ is what runs on the change: it calls the notification
  script, and then makes sure the radio's player is running. The stream is
  continuous, so a player that is already playing carries the next song by
  itself - restarting it would only cost a reconnect, a fresh buffer and the
  silence while both happen.

The radio comes from ~rmpc-audio-stream-url~, which is a parameter: point it at
another stream and the next song change starts that one instead.

** The theme

~rmpc-theme~ is written into RMPC's themes directory and selected by the rendered
config, so a palette switch repaints the player down to the volume slider and the
visualiser's bars, with the player following the same ink and accent as the
terminal it is drawn in. [[file:palettes.org][Palettes]] has the colours; the
player is a surface like any other.

** What is not here

Nix is not in this stack and never was: MPD, RMPC and Cava come from the
distribution's own packages, and a session that cannot reach a store still plays
music. The bar's "now playing" reading is a script the shell runs on a two-second
interval - a machine that is not playing anything shows an icon and no text,
rather than an error where a song would be.
